# Assignment 1: mesh basics

Geometric Modeling | C++17, Eigen and libigl

## Objective

Explore triangle-mesh connectivity, compare shading normals, and implement iterative sqrt(3) subdivision. Test with `cube.obj`, `sphere.obj` and `bunny-small.off` from `data/`.

Students implement computational code only. The teacher provides loading, visualization, controls, normal adaptation, export, diagnostics and reset. Do not modify viewer integration or add graphics dependencies to computational code. The untouched starter displays teacher preview normals; this preview is not evidence that your normal computation is implemented.

## What you implement

Work in `skeleton/a01.cpp`. The comments in [a01.hpp](../include/gm/a01.hpp) and the corresponding skeleton functions specify inputs, outputs and numerical conventions. Consult them when implementing each function.

1. **Neighborhoods.** Compute the incident faces and distinct edge neighbors of every vertex, including isolated vertices. You may implement these relations directly or use libigl adjacency helpers.
2. **Shading normals.** Compute face normals for flat shading, area-weighted vertex normals for smooth shading, and area-weighted corner normals controlled by a crease angle. Libigl normal helpers are permitted; understand their weighting and threshold conventions.
3. **sqrt(3) subdivision.** Implement face-barycenter insertion, old-vertex relocation and original-edge flipping. The supplied `subdivide_sqrt3` combines your three stages into a complete step that can be repeated. All stages read the original mesh for that step. Implement the subdivision rules yourself; core topology and barycenter helpers are permitted, but ready-made subdivision routines are not.

For subdivision, use **watertight, consistently oriented triangular vertex-manifold meshes without isolated vertices**. Boundary handling is not required. Neighborhood and shading computations may use open meshes and isolated vertices. Supplied validation reports unsupported inputs.

The teacher renderer handles the association between numerical normals and displayed corners. Mesh duplication for shading and connected-component coloring are not student tasks.

## Experiments

Print both adjacency relations for `bunny-small.off`. Check selected entries against the input triangles and explain how duplicate neighbors are avoided.

Compare flat, vertex and corner shading on the cube and sphere, and inspect the bunny. Try corner crease angles **0, 45, 90 and 180 degrees**. Explain sharp features, smoothing and the influence of triangulation on area-weighted normals. Use screenshots labeled with model, normal association and crease angle.

Inspect the intermediate **Split only** and **Split + relocate** views, resetting before each comparison. Then compare one, two and three full subdivision iterations on the cube, sphere and bunny. Explain the changes in shape and connectivity. Include intermediate-stage screenshots and screenshots labeled by full iteration number. Recompute shading after refinement when inspecting your normals on the refined mesh.

Design independent numerical checks. Verify vertex/face counts, winding, edge incidence and preservation of Euler characteristic; also check that translating or uniformly scaling the input has the corresponding effect on subdivision. Use the supplied public checks as a starting point; passing them alone does not establish correctness.

Reference: L. Kobbelt, [sqrt(3)-Subdivision, SIGGRAPH 2000](https://www.graphics.rwth-aachen.de/media/papers/sqrt31.pdf).
